two kinds of noise

cheap at the network, expensive at the model

noise has to exist at two places. the network layer hides when and from where you send. the endpoint layer hides which prompts are real. only the second one costs real inference.

NZ.NETPLANNED

network noise

cost: low · no inference

Fake packets from the client into the mixnet, thrown away at the last node. They protect against a provider that works with the entry node and plays with timing to match requests to people.

  • where it endslast mixnet hop
  • reaches the modelno
  • who paysthe sender, cheaply
NZ.ENDv0.1 GENERATOR

endpoint noise

cost: ~$0.001-0.005 per decoy on a cheap model · ~10x on a top model

Decoy requests that really reach the model, so even the SI provider can not tell which ones came from a person. This is the expensive layer, and it is what the Noise Pool mainly pays for.

  • reaches the modelyes
  • who paysthe Noise Pool, to miners

proof of noise

pay for decoys without learning which they are

miners commit to a batch before sending it, and a random sample is opened afterwards. one epoch is one hour. commit, sample and open already run in the lab; stake and payouts come with the network.

commit.send.

01PLANNED

stake

The miner locks a stake before taking part.

e.g. 0.1 ETH
02v0.1 LOCAL

commit

At the start of each epoch, the miner publishes a hash of the decoy batch it is about to send.

epoch = 1 hour
03v0.1 LOCAL

sample

At the end, the hash of a future block picks about 1% of the batch at random.

no one knows the sample in advance
04v0.1 LOCAL

open

The miner reveals the sampled decoys. Checked: they were sent (their nullifier shows up at the gateway) and they look credible.

two checks
05PLANNED

pay or slash

Pass: paid for the whole batch. Fail: the stake is lost.

[nz.09]

known risks

open problems, written down

none of these are solved. each one has a planned answer, and each answer still has to be tested.

RK.01OPEN

self-dealing

A miner who also runs a gateway could "send" decoys to itself and get paid for nothing.

planned answer: decoys go to several gateways at random, and gateways stake too.
RK.02OPEN

noise detectors

A provider can train a model to spot decoys. This becomes an arms race, not a one-time fix.

planned answer: the decoy generator copies real conversation patterns and is updated over time.
RK.03OPEN

provider terms

Automated fake traffic to closed SI providers may break their terms of use.

planned answer: start on open-source models run by partner gateways.
RK.04OPEN

cost

Endpoint decoys cost real inference. If funding dries up, the noise level drops and so does privacy.

planned answer: fee on real requests plus a bootstrap fund, with the cost shown openly.
